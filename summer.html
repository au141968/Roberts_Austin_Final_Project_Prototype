Aquila

This constellation is known as The Eagle, and includes the star, Altair which is one of our closest stars. Look north to slightly northwest at a 45 degree angle above the horizon. Look for a bright white star, (Altair with two dimmer stars on either side.) The three stars form the head of the eagle. According to Greek Mythology, Aquila was Zeus's eagle.

×

Cygnus

This constellation is known as The Swan, and includes the star, Deneb in the tail. Deneb is a very bright northern star. Look for the Big Dipper in the northern sky, and look slightly northwest from the Big Dipper's handle. The neck is made up of a straight line of stars. Orpheus was a musician and voyaged with Jason and the Argonauts in Greek Mythology. He was changed into a swan, after his death and set into the sky, next to his lyre, the constellation Lyra.

×

Lyra

The Lyre contains another bright star, Vega, which is at the top of the constellation. Look north to find Vega, then look for a parallelogram below it. This is the body of the lyre. It is very close to the head of the constellation, Cygnus the Swan. According to Greek Mythology, Orpheus received a magical lyre from the god, Apollo. After Orpheus's tragic end, Zeus positioned his lyre next to him in the stars.

The Summer Triangle is easy to spot in the northern sky and the brightest star from each of these three summer constellations make up this triangle. Vega, Deneb, and Altair.
