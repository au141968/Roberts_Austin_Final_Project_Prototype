Orion

Known as the Hunter, Orion is a very visible constellation in the winter sky. Facing east, look for the 3 bright stars in a row that make up Orion's belt. Then look further from the belt to see the hourglass shape that makes up the body of Orion. Two significant stars are present in this constellation: the red giant Betelgeuse is on the upper left in the hourglass, and Rigel, a blue-white supergiant star is to the lower left of the belt. In Greek Mythology, Orion is the son of the god Poseidon. He became a great hunter, but attacked the goddess Artemis, who was also a great huntress. Orion was turned into a constellation as a result of this audacity.

Gemini

The Twins constellation is best favored by finding Orion's belt in the eastern-southeastern sky. Look above Orion's belt to find two bright stars to either side of Orion's shoulders. These are Pollux and Castor, the heads of the twins. According to Greek Mythology, the twins were Leda's sons, but had two different fathers. Pollux was the son of Zeus, and Castor the son of a mortal king. The two were inseparable in life. They joined Jason and the Argonauts in the quest to find the Golden Fleece. Castor was killed in a battle. Pollux was so upset that he requested to be placed in the sky with his dead brother. Zeus honored his request. As they were in life, Pollux and Castor are inseparable in the sky.

Taurus

To locate Taurus the Bull, find Orion's belt. Follow the belt up to a bright orange-red star. This is Aldebaran which became one of the bull's eyes. In the nearby space of Aldebaran you will see the Pleides, a group of seven stars that make the backs of the bull. In Greek Mythology, Zeus turned himself into a beautiful white bull to lure Europa, a mortal princess to come with him to Crete. There, Europa gave birth to Minos, who later became king.
